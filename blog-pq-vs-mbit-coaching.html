PQ vs mBIT Coaching: Which Approach Fits?

Positive Intelligence and mBIT coaching are complementary, but they solve different kinds of stuckness.

By Claudina Hafenscher

COMENSA Accredited Senior Coach & Certified PQ® Coach

What is the difference between PQ and mBIT coaching?

Positive Intelligence coaching builds mental fitness by helping you recognise saboteur patterns and recover faster into clearer, wiser responses. mBIT coaching helps you align head, heart, and gut intelligence so decisions feel congruent. PQ is especially useful for repeated self-sabotage under stress, while mBIT is useful when logic, values, and instinct are pulling in different directions.

How the two methods work together

Many clients need both mental fitness and embodied alignment. PQ gives you repeatable daily practice for shifting state. mBIT helps you listen to the different intelligences involved in a decision before acting.

  • Choose PQ when stress, self-doubt, judging, pleasing, or over-achieving patterns are the main blocker.
  • Choose mBIT when your head says one thing, your heart says another, and your gut does not feel safe yet.
  • Use a discovery session to decide whether one method or an integrated coaching path is the best fit.

Side-by-side comparison

PQ (Positive Intelligence) mBIT (Multi-Brain Integration)
Core focus Mental fitness — recognising and weakening saboteur patterns Aligning head, heart, and gut intelligence into one decision
Best for Repeated self-sabotage, stress reactivity, judging, pleasing, over-achieving Conflicted decisions where logic, values, and instinct disagree
Typical format A structured programme with daily practice reps between sessions Facilitated sessions working through a specific decision or pattern
Used together Common combination: PQ builds the daily capacity to catch a saboteur in the moment; mBIT is then used to work through the specific decision the saboteur was distorting.

Common questions

Can I do both PQ and mBIT coaching?

Yes — many clients start with PQ to build baseline mental fitness, then bring mBIT in when a specific high-stakes decision needs head/heart/gut alignment.

Which approach is better for burnout recovery?

PQ is usually the starting point, since burnout often shows up as chronic saboteur activation (over-achieving, pleasing, judging). mBIT can follow once the immediate stress response has settled, to work through what changes need to happen next.

Which approach is better for a career or leadership decision?

mBIT is built for exactly this — decisions where your head, heart, and gut are pulling in different directions and you need a structured way to listen to all three before choosing.
