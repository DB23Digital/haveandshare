What Does Coaching Actually Do About Imposter Syndrome? A Practical Breakdown

Claudina Hafenscher

COMENSA Accredited Senior Coach & Certified PQ® Coach

11 August 2026 9 min read

Does coaching actually work for imposter syndrome?

Yes, when it goes beyond reassurance into repeatable practice. Coaching identifies the specific Saboteur pattern driving the self-doubt and builds daily mental fitness habits that intercept it in real time, rather than offering generic encouragement that fades within a day.

Explore PQ coaching Explore individual coaching

"Does coaching actually work for this?" is the honest question underneath most enquiries about imposter syndrome, and it deserves an honest, specific answer rather than a testimonial. Here is what the work actually looks like, session by session, so you can judge for yourself whether it is the right next step.

Why understanding alone does not fix it

Most people who reach out already understand imposter syndrome intellectually. They have read the articles, they can explain the concept to a colleague, and they still feel it every Monday morning. That gap, between understanding a pattern and being free of it, is the entire reason coaching exists as distinct from simply reading about the problem.

The patterns driving self-doubt do not live in the part of the brain that reads articles. They live in habituated neural pathways that fire automatically, faster than conscious thought can intervene. Coaching works because it trains a different, faster response in the same pathway, through repetition, not through one more explanation.

1

Mapping your specific Saboteur pattern

The first session is not a generic conversation about confidence. We identify which Saboteurs are most active for you specifically, usually the Judge paired with one or two accomplices such as the Hyper-Achiever, the Hyper-Rational, or the Controller, using a structured assessment alongside conversation. This matters because generic advice fails precisely because it is generic; the intervention that helps a Hyper-Achiever is not the intervention that helps a Pleaser.

2

Building the interception habit

Once the pattern is named, the work becomes practical: catching the Saboteur voice in the moment it fires, before it has fully taken over. This is trained through short, daily mental fitness exercises, PQ® Reps, that physically shift your nervous system out of threat-response and into a calmer, clearer state. It takes seconds per rep, several times a day, and it is the part of the work that actually produces change, not the conversation about it.

3

Working with the body, not just the thinking mind

Imposter syndrome is somatic as much as cognitive: the racing heart before a presentation, the tight gut before a difficult meeting. Through mBIT, we work with the head, heart, and gut as three distinct centres of intelligence, so the shift happens in the body as well as in your thinking, which is why it holds under real pressure rather than only in the calm of a coaching session.

4

Accountability between sessions

Change happens in the week between sessions, not in the session itself. Each conversation reviews what was practiced, what triggered the old pattern, and what worked or didn't, so the daily practice actually compounds instead of fading the way New Year's resolutions do.

What changes, and roughly when

Nobody can promise a universal timeline, because the depth of the pattern varies. What research on the Positive Intelligence programme does show consistently is that measurable shifts in mental fitness typically appear after six to eight weeks of consistent daily practice. Clients usually describe the early change as noticing the Saboteur voice earlier, before it derails a decision, rather than the voice disappearing entirely. The deeper, more durable change, where the Sage becomes the default rather than the exception, builds over months of continued practice.

This is worth saying plainly: coaching does not remove the Saboteurs. It cannot, and any programme promising that is overselling. What it does is weaken their grip and strengthen your capacity to operate from clarity instead of fear, consistently enough that it becomes your default rather than a good day.

How this differs from therapy or generic confidence coaching

Coaching is forward-facing and skills-based. It does not require excavating your childhood or dwelling in difficulty, and it is not a substitute for therapy where deeper clinical support is needed; the two work well alongside each other. It also differs from generic confidence coaching, which tends to focus on mindset affirmations and public-facing presence. This work goes underneath that, to the specific pattern generating the doubt in the first place, so the confidence that results is not a performance but an actual internal shift.

If you are still deciding

The most useful next step is usually a direct conversation rather than more reading. A free discovery session is not a sales pitch; it is a chance to describe what you are actually experiencing and hear, specifically, what the coaching would involve for your pattern, before committing to anything.
