Team Alignment

Team Alignment Workshop Agenda for Leaders

A clear view of the facilitation process helps leadership teams understand what they are committing to before they enquire.

By Claudina Hafenscher

COMENSA Accredited Senior Coach & Certified PQ® Coach

What happens in a team alignment workshop?

A team alignment workshop starts before the room gathers. The facilitator clarifies the leadership challenge, listens for hidden patterns, designs a focused session, guides honest conversation during the workshop, and helps the team convert agreements into behaviours and follow-up actions.

Before, during, and after the session

A strong workshop is not a motivational event. It is a structured process for surfacing patterns, creating shared language, and setting practical commitments.

  • Before the session, clarify the business issue, stakeholders, desired outcomes, and any sensitive team dynamics.
  • During the session, create safety, surface patterns, align expectations, and agree on behaviours the team can practise.
  • After the session, document actions, review follow-through, and decide whether coaching or Reality Check measurement is needed.

A typical workshop agenda

Phase What happens
Pre-work (before the room) Scoping call with the sponsor, brief 1:1 check-ins or a short survey to surface real issues in advance
Opening Set ground rules, establish psychological safety, name the purpose of the session honestly
Core facilitation Structured exercises to surface patterns, name what's unsaid, and build shared language for the issue
Commitments Team agrees on specific, observable behaviours — not vague intentions
Follow-up Written summary of agreements, a check-in point, and optionally a Reality Check measurement to track whether behaviour actually shifted

Exact timing (half-day vs. full-day vs. a short series of shorter sessions) is scoped to the issue and team size during the initial call, not fixed in advance.

Choosing the right format for your team

  • Half-day session: a specific, well-defined issue with a team that already has reasonable trust.
  • Full-day workshop: a bigger or longer-standing issue, or a team that needs more time to build safety before real conversation happens.
  • A short series over weeks: deeper dysfunction, new leadership, or where behaviour change needs reinforcement rather than a single event.

Common questions

How many people can attend a workshop?

This is scoped to your team size during the initial call — facilitation design (breakout structure, timing) adjusts accordingly.

Is this in-person or online?

Both are available. In-person tends to work best for the core facilitation phase; online works well for scoping calls, shorter check-ins, and geographically spread teams.

What follow-up is included after the workshop?

A written summary of agreed commitments and a follow-up check-in are standard. Reality Check measurement is available as an add-on where the team wants to track whether behaviour actually changed.
